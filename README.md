CETA Community Digital Infrastructure Platform

Building Community Networks By Us and For Us.

Developing open-source, community-managed digital infrastructure for meaningful digital inclusion in refugee and underserved communities.

1. Project Overview

The CETA Community Digital Infrastructure Platform is a refugee-led technology innovation initiative by the Community Empowerment & Transformation Agency (CETA), based in Rhino Camp Refugee Settlement, Uganda.

The project seeks to design and develop an integrated, affordable and replicable digital infrastructure model that brings together community connectivity management, offline-first AI-powered learning, local content delivery and transparent internet access management.

It builds on CETA's practical experience establishing and supporting community connectivity hubs in Siripi and Ofua, where refugees and host communities are gaining access to internet connectivity, digital learning opportunities and locally managed digital services.

Rather than treating connectivity as an end in itself, this initiative explores how communities can build, manage and benefit from the digital infrastructure they depend on.

Our ambition is to move beyond providing internet access towards enabling communities to own, manage and develop their own digital ecosystems.

2. The Challenge

Millions of people living in refugee settlements and underserved communities remain excluded from meaningful participation in the digital world.

Even where internet infrastructure becomes available, several challenges persist:

The cost of accessing internet services remains a barrier.

Educational resources are often inaccessible without reliable internet connectivity.

Communities have limited locally managed digital infrastructure.

Internet access management can be fragmented and difficult to sustain.

Offline educational and digital services remain underdeveloped.

Refugees and underserved populations are frequently positioned as technology consumers rather than technology designers and builders.

The challenge is therefore not simply the absence of internet connectivity. It is the absence of integrated, affordable and community-managed digital ecosystems that enable meaningful and sustainable digital participation.

3. Existing Foundation: From Community Hubs to Digital Infrastructure Innovation

CETA's experience in Rhino Camp provides the practical foundation for this initiative.

Through partnerships and collaboration with the Internet Society Foundation, Internet Society Uganda Chapter, Hello World, RENU, OPM, UNHCR and other stakeholders, CETA has participated in establishing and supporting community connectivity and digital learning infrastructure.

Two important implementation environments are:

Siripi Community Connectivity Hub

Community-based internet access.

Digital skills development.

Internet access management and voucher experience.

Access to digital learning opportunities.

Ofua Community Connectivity Hub

Community connectivity and digital inclusion.

Engagement with students and surrounding communities.

Practical experience in internet access management.

Opportunities to develop and test locally relevant digital services.

These hubs provide real-world environments for understanding infrastructure, affordability, user management, community participation and sustainability.

They are the foundation of the proposed innovation, not the final innovation itself.

The next stage is to transform the lessons, operational experience and community needs emerging from these hubs into an integrated and reusable technology platform.

4. The Proposed Innovation

The project aims to develop four interconnected technological components.

4.1 Community Connectivity Management

Develop an open-source management layer that supports community-managed connectivity infrastructure.

Proposed capabilities include:

Network and access administration.

User registration and management.

Connectivity monitoring.

Integration with supported network equipment.

Community-level usage reporting.

Flexible internet access allocation.

4.2 Offline-First AI Learning Environment

Develop an offline-first digital learning environment that makes educational resources and AI-assisted learning capabilities accessible in low-connectivity settings.

Proposed capabilities include:

Locally hosted educational resources.

Offline access to learning materials.

Locally accessible AI-assisted learning tools.

Support for self-directed and guided learning.

Integration with existing digital learning resources.

Synchronisation of selected resources when internet connectivity becomes available.

The aim is to reduce dependence on continuous internet connectivity while expanding access to meaningful digital learning opportunities.

4.3 Open-Source Internet Voucher Management

Develop a transparent and flexible internet access management application informed by CETA's practical experience operating community connectivity hubs.

Proposed capabilities include:

Voucher generation and management.

User registration and authentication.

Flexible access packages and validity periods.

Integration with supported network authentication systems.

Usage tracking and reporting.

Basic revenue and transaction records.

Administrative dashboards.

The objective is to improve transparency, accountability, affordability and operational sustainability in community-managed connectivity environments.

4.4 Local Digital Services and Content Delivery

Develop a locally hosted digital services environment that enables communities to access relevant information and resources without requiring continuous external internet connectivity.

Proposed capabilities include:

Local content hosting.

Educational resource distribution.

Community information services.

Offline knowledge repositories.

Local access to selected digital applications.

Synchronisation with external services when connectivity is available.

5. Proposed Technical Architecture

The platform will be designed as a modular system connecting the following layers:

Layer 1: Connectivity Infrastructure

Internet backhaul.

Community routers and access points.

Local area network infrastructure.

User access points.

Layer 2: Local Infrastructure

Local server.

Content hosting.

Local application environment.

Offline resource storage.

Layer 3: Digital Applications

Internet voucher management.

User administration.

Offline AI learning.

Digital content delivery.

Layer 4: Community Management

Community administrators.

User access management.

Usage monitoring.

Operational reporting.

The components will be designed to work together while remaining modular enough to support independent deployment and adaptation.

Detailed architecture diagrams, software choices, interfaces and integration specifications will be documented as development progresses.

6. Design Principles

The project will be guided by the following principles:

Community ownership: Communities should participate in designing, operating and managing their digital infrastructure.

Open source: Technology should be reusable, adaptable and accessible.

Offline-first: Essential digital services should remain useful when internet connectivity is unavailable.

Affordability: Solutions should consider the economic realities of underserved communities.

Interoperability: Components should integrate with appropriate existing technologies and infrastructure.

Security and privacy: User information and digital services should be protected through responsible technical design.

Scalability: The architecture should support adaptation across different community environments.

Local capacity development: Communities should develop the skills needed to maintain and improve their systems.

7. Development Roadmap

The project will follow an incremental development approach.

Phase 1: Research and System Design

Document the existing Siripi and Ofua infrastructure.

Identify technical and operational requirements.

Map existing network and service environments.

Engage potential users and community administrators.

Develop the initial system architecture.

Phase 2: Core Platform Development

Develop the internet voucher management application.

Establish the local server environment.

Develop the initial user administration interface.

Document network integration requirements.

Phase 3: Offline AI and Learning Integration

Establish an offline-first learning environment.

Evaluate suitable open-source AI technologies.

Integrate locally hosted learning resources.

Test access to AI-assisted learning without continuous internet connectivity.

Phase 4: Integration and Field Testing

Integrate the platform components.

Test functionality across the different system layers.

Evaluate usability, reliability and security.

8. Expected Outputs

The intended outputs include:

An integrated community digital infrastructure prototype.

An open-source internet voucher management application.

An offline-first AI-assisted learning environment.

A locally hosted digital content and services platform.

10. About CETA

The Community Empowerment & Transformation Agency (CETA) is a refugee-led community-based organisation established in 2023 by refugees and host-community youth in Rhino Camp Refugee Settlement, Uganda.

CETA works to expand meaningful digital inclusion through community-centred connectivity, digital skills development, innovation, education and livelihood opportunities.

Our approach recognises that refugees and underserved communities should not remain passive recipients of technology. They should be empowered to design, build, manage and shape the digital systems that influence their lives.

Our vision is a future in which communities are not simply connected to the internet but have the knowledge, tools and capacity to shape their own digital futures.
